01  What Kairos is
Kairos is a clock app that remembers your life. It ships every time instrument a phone should have
— world clock, alarm, timer, stopwatch, focus sessions — and adds the layer those instruments
have never carried: a living timeline of the moments ahead of you and the moments behind you.
Phone clocks tell you what time it is. They say nothing about what the time is for. A person counting down to a
wedding, a result day, a flight home, or counting up from the day a parent died, is holding that number in their
head and losing it daily. Kairos holds it instead, and puts it where a phone is actually looked at: the home screen.

Three conclusions follow, and they set the whole specification. First, the moments model has to be the product,
not a tab inside a clock — because that is where the emotional pull is and where the existing apps stop. Second,
the instruments must be genuinely good, because a clock app that loses an alarm is deleted the same week.
Third, the widget is the product surface, not a shortcut: in this category the home screen is where the app is
actually used.
03  One object, three tenses
Every moment in Kairos is a single record that moves through three tenses on its own. It counts
down before it happens, becomes ongoing while it lasts, and counts up forever afterwards —
without the user creating a second entry, duplicating a date, or archiving anything.

Why one object and not three
A birthday is not three things. It is one date that is 200 days away, then today, then 1 day ago. Apps that model
countdowns and count-ups as separate features force the user to re-enter the same event twice and then
remember to switch it over. Modelling direction as a property of time rather than a property of the record removes
that work entirely, and it makes the zero moment — the instant the number reaches zero — a designed event
rather than a dead end.
The information architecture that follows
Three tabs, not eight. Next holds everything ahead, Now holds anything in progress plus the live instruments
(timer, stopwatch, focus), Then holds everything behind. Clock, alarm and world time live in one Instruments area
reachable from Now. The user learns the model once and never has to think about where a thing is.
04  The design language
The visual target is the standard set by the best Apple system apps: a near-black canvas, one
enormous number, and nothing else competing for attention. Ten rules govern every screen, and
they are enforced in the design tokens so no screen can drift.

Milestones the app notices for you
Round numbers land on their own and the app offers them as a card rather than forcing them into the timeline:
100, 500, 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 days; 1,000 weeks; 10,000 hours; the 100th workout; one year into a streak;
half-birthdays; and the quietly devastating one — the day you have been married as long as you were single.
Shared moments
A moment can be shared by link with up to 20 people. Both sides see the same countdown, the same theme and
the same zero moment, and a shared widget can show it on two home screens at once. There is no feed, no
follower graph and no public profile: sharing is a private link between people who already know each other, and
that is the entire social layer.

Templates, written for India first
A template library removes the blank-screen problem: board exams for class 10 and 12, NEET, JEE, UPSC

The zero moment
When a countdown reaches zero the app takes the whole screen: the photo full-bleed, the number dissolving into
the title, an animation chosen by category, one haptic pattern, optional sound, and a share card offered once and
never again. Then the moment flips to counting up on its own. This is the app's signature thirty seconds, and it is
the screen that gets posted.

Every widget instance is configured independently: which moment, which design, which theme, which font, and
whether it counts in sleeps or days. A user can run six widgets showing six different moments, and each one
deep-links to its own screen on tap.
How the digits stay live
iOS rations widget refreshes to a handful per hour, which is far too few for a countdown that ticks. Kairos renders
the moving text with the platform's own timer text — WidgetKit's timer style on iOS, Chronometer on Android —
so the digits advance without a single refresh request. Only the label, the photo and the theme need a refresh,
and those change rarely. This is the difference between a widget that is correct when you glance at it and one
showing a number from four hours ago.

12
Why not native on both platforms
SwiftUI plus Jetpack Compose has the higher ceiling, particularly for watch complications, and costs roughly 1.8
times the effort with two native developers instead of one. Choose it only if the budget supports a team of three
and widgets are judged worth more than half the product. For a first release, Flutter plus native widget extensions
reaches the same visual quality on the surfaces users actually see.
One further constraint is worth stating plainly: Flutter does not render inside a widget or a Live Activity. The widget
UI is a native extension on each platform and Flutter only passes data across the bridge. Budget two to three
weeks of Swift and Kotlin work for the widget layer alone, and treat it as a first-class workstream rather than a
plugin install.
